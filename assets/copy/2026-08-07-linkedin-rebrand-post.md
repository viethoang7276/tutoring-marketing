# LinkedIn — rebrand / launch post

**Status:** DRAFT v2, awaiting Hoang. Not published.
**Channel:** LinkedIn company page (`linkedin.com/company/edlintics`).
**Angle:** lead with the insight (textbook entries vs. the real document trail), let the rebrand
follow as its consequence. Nobody engages with "we are rebranding."

---

An exam hands you the transaction. Real work makes you prove it happened.

That gap is most of what I've spent the last eight years learning.

I've taught accounting to more than 400 students across 20+ schools, and I've been running companies the whole time. The two don't look the same from the inside.

In a course, cost is five lines on a page. In a real company it's thousands of materials across thousands of items, and someone has to work out which cost belongs where before any of it turns into an entry.

And the entry is the last step. Before it there's a contract, a purchase order, an invoice, and a delivery record proving what actually arrived — because you can't price what you can't prove was delivered. Then a tax return that has to agree with all of it.

The audits I've sat through were about that trail. Not about whether I could post a journal entry.

There's a second thing students aren't being told. Most of that entry work is being automated now, and it should be. I'm building toward an AI bookkeeping tool myself, for founders and small businesses who shouldn't have to do this by hand.

But software doesn't know when it's wrong. It will categorise a cost confidently and incorrectly, and it has no idea that the delivery note doesn't match the invoice. Someone still has to catch that.

That's the part people have backwards. Understanding the fundamentals didn't become less important because AI can do the entries. It's what lets you use the tool at all, and audit what comes out of it.

So that's what Edlintics is now: interactive lessons that work a concept until it holds, exam-targeted practice, the real documents an entry sits on, and how to work with AI without trusting it blindly.

It's for two people. The student who needs to pass in October and be useful in January. And the founder who doesn't want to do the accounting, but does need to read their own numbers well enough to run the company.

Edlintics opens on 1 September.

## Notes before posting

- **Verify one line.** "The audits I've sat through were about that trail." It carries the most
  weight in the post and is the likeliest to draw a comment. Soften if it is not accurate to the
  actual experience.
- **Keep the AI paragraphs in this order.** The "I'm building one myself" admission comes *before*
  "software doesn't know when it's wrong." Reversed, the passage reads as an incumbent defending
  turf; in this order it reads as someone building the thing telling you why you still need the
  fundamentals.
- **AI product stays direction, not promise.** No date, no feature list. 25 days to launch with a
  15-customer validation target on the €99 courses — a second product with a roadmap splits the
  story and invites "when can I have it."
- **Voice:** no emoji, no exclamation points, no hype (locked brand rules). It will read
  understated next to the rest of the LinkedIn feed. That is the point.
- **Hashtags:** optional and low-value here. If used, max three
  (`#accounting #financialliteracy #edtech`). Recommend none.
- **Do NOT pre-empt the competitive question in the post.** See the comment-ready reply below.

## Comment-ready reply — "what stops Brilliant / Duolingo copying this?"

> Fair question. Both are horizontal platforms that need millions of users and run on daily-habit
> mechanics. This is seasonal, syllabus-specific work — matching content to an actual module at an
> actual school — which doesn't scale horizontally and isn't worth their time.
>
> The real competition isn't Brilliant. It's the free YouTube explainer, and it's already there.
> What it doesn't give you is whether it matches your exam, the documents underneath the entry, or
> someone to sit with you when you're stuck.

Short verbal version: *the platforms can copy the lessons; they can't copy knowing your syllabus and
answering the phone.*
