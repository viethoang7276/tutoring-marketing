# Effects of AI-content labels on reach and engagement for short-form video (TikTok, Reels, Facebook, YouTube Shorts)

Research date: 27 Oct 2026. Evidence is graded: **[Platform]** official statement, **[Peer-reviewed]**, **[Preprint/working paper]**, **[Vendor/agency]** commercial research with an interest in the result, **[Anecdotal]** creator blog or opinion.

## 1. Have TikTok, Meta or YouTube stated whether labelled content is down-ranked?

### Takeaway
YouTube says plainly, in its own words, that the label is not used in ranking or monetisation. TikTok's help centre reportedly says the label "won't affect the distribution" of a video, but I could only see that quote through secondary sources. Meta has published no statement that labelled posts lose reach. It has said only that AI-*profile* accounts that fail to self-label "may have their reach limited", while accounts that do label "will not see a change". All three platforms aim their real distribution penalties at non-disclosure, mass-produced or templated "inauthentic" content, and AI spam accounts, not at the label itself.

### Cited Findings
- **[Platform] YouTube, March 2024 creator Q&A:** "The YouTube discovery system (the algorithm) won't be using the disclosure labels when determining recommendations or ranking." And: "the labels won't have any effect on a video's ability to be monetized on YouTube." — [YouTube Official Blog, Rene's Top Five, 21 Mar 2024](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-march-21-2024-edition/)
- **[Platform] YouTube help page:** "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." Creators who repeatedly fail to disclose "may be subject to manual application of a label, or penalties from YouTube." — [YouTube Help: Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **[Platform] What YouTube does not require you to disclose:** "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs", using generative AI for an "outline, script, thumbnail, title", "Caption creation", "Idea generation", and clearly unrealistic content such as "Someone riding a unicorn through a fantastical world". Disclosure is aimed at realistic content, for example content that "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do". The page does not say directly whether a generic synthetic narrator that imitates no real person needs a label. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **[Secondary report of a platform change]** Coverage of YouTube's 2026 label overhaul (more prominent placement, automatic detection from May 2026) says YouTube repeated that labels won't affect recommendations or monetisation. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/youtube-will-try-to-automatically-flag-ai-videos-starting-this-month/)
- **[Platform policy, separate]** YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy for mass-produced or templated content affects monetisation eligibility. It is separate from the AI disclosure label. — [AIR Media-Tech glossary (secondary)](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtubes-ai-content-disclosure-policy)
- **[Platform, quoted second-hand] TikTok:** "Turning on the AI-generated content setting won't affect the distribution of your video as long as it doesn't violate our Community Guidelines." I could not load TikTok's help page (support.tiktok.com redirects to a page that rendered empty for me), so this quote comes from third-party pages that cite it. — [Jack Righteous blog](https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/ai-art-visuals-creatives/tiktok-ai-music-video-labels-spam-reach-2026); [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/how-to-label-ai-content-on-tiktok-without-losing-reach/)
- **[Platform policy via press] TikTok:** creators must label AI-generated content "that contains realistic images, audio or video". — [ABC17 / AP coverage](https://abc17news.com/?p=1219129)
- **[Platform via press] TikTok spam enforcement, July 2026:** TikTok is testing better detection of *accounts dedicated to* AI-generated spam that crowds out original creators. This targets accounts, not individual labelled posts. — [Tubefilter, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/14/tiktok-ai-transparency-tools-content-labels/amp/)
- **[Platform feature via press] TikTok viewer-side control, Nov 2025:** a "Manage topics" slider lets each user see more or less AI-generated content in For You. It cannot switch AI content off entirely. Detection relies on C2PA Content Credentials and invisible watermarking. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-adds-option-to-limit-ai-content-in-your-for-you-feed/805952/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/tiktok-ai-transparency/)
- **[Platform via press] Instagram AI-generated profile label:** "Creators who proactively add the AI-generated profile label will not see a change to their account's reach". Accounts Instagram identifies as AI-generated profiles that don't label themselves "may have their reach limited". This account-level label is separate from the per-post "AI info" label. — [FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/490973/instagram-ai-creator-label-ai-generated-profile.html/amp)
- **[Platform] Meta transparency page:** describes how and how often "AI info" labels are applied (for example, over 130M Facebook reels with label views in a 29-day window in Oct 2024) but says nothing about ranking. — [Meta Transparency Center: Labeling AI Content](https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content)
- **[Executive statement via press] Adam Mosseri (Instagram):** "I don't think we should filter out AI content… I think we should let you know if content is AI content or not". In his 31 Dec 2025 post he wrote: "Label AI content, verify real content, and continue improving ranking systems that reward originality." Secondary coverage says Instagram changed its ranking in Dec 2025 to deprioritise "overly templated or automated" material. — [ainauten (Lenny's Podcast)](https://news.ainauten.com/en/story/instagram-chef-mosseri-will-ai-inhalte-nicht-aus-dem-feed-verbannen); [Netinfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-launches-ai-creator-labels-to-flag-accounts-that-regularly-use-ai-generated-content/)

### Inferences
- No platform says the label itself lowers ranking. The documented reach risks are (a) failing to disclose when disclosure is required, (b) templated or mass-produced "unoriginal" output (YouTube, Instagram), and (c) on TikTok since Nov 2025, individual viewers turning down AI content with the slider. That last one is an indirect channel by which labelled or detected AI content could reach fewer people, even with no algorithmic penalty.
- Per YouTube's rules, a non-realistic illustrated explainer with a generic AI narrator (no real person imitated) likely falls outside required disclosure. TikTok's rule targets "realistic" audio too. This is my reading of the policy text, not a platform ruling.

### Gaps
- I could not read TikTok's own help-centre text verbatim. Its "won't affect distribution" wording is confirmed only through third parties.
- I found no Meta primary statement, for or against, on whether the per-post "AI info" label affects Reels or Facebook distribution.
- No platform states whether its built-in text-to-speech voices trigger an automatic AI label.

## 2. Peer-reviewed and preprint studies (2023-2026) on AI-disclosure labels and engagement, trust, sharing and credibility

### Takeaway
The best evidence is the 2026 *Journal of Consumer Research* (JCR) paper by Carney, Riveros and Tully. In its TikTok data, disclosed posts got about 7-8% fewer likes and about 7% less engagement *per view*. In its experiments the penalty was small (d about 0.2-0.26 overall) and larger among viewers who noticed the label (d about 0.45-0.63). It runs through weaker parasocial connection, because viewers infer that the creator put in less effort, and effort-signalling disclosures reduced it. Credibility studies mostly find that "AI-generated" labels lower the perceived accuracy and shareability of *true* content. A CHI 2025 study (N=911) found no significant overall change in liking, commenting or sharing. The evidence points to a small but real per-view engagement cost, not a collapse.

### Cited Findings
- **[Peer-reviewed] Carney, Riveros & Tully, "Made With AI: Consumer Engagement With Social Media Containing AI Disclosures", *JCR*, published 7 May 2026** (earlier SSRN 4988760, 2025):
  - Study 1 (TikTok archival data): 1,135,817 posts by 8,650 random US TikTokers, June 2023 to April 2024, around TikTok's September 2023 disclosure policy. After the policy, 2,749 posts carried a disclosure. The label is added automatically when in-app AI effects are used, and by creators when they used external tools.
  - Conditional on views, disclosed posts got about 7-8% fewer likes (coefficients -0.080 and -0.084) and about 7% less total engagement (-0.070 to -0.073).
  - Disclosed posts also had fewer views on average (mean 4,925 vs 11,902), but the authors do not claim this is causal and state that "the archival nature of the data still limits the causal interpretation".
  - Experiments: Study 3 d = .21 overall (p = .079, marginal) and d = .45 among those who noticed the label. Study 4 partial η² = .017 for the platform label and .021 for creator self-disclosure. Study 5 d = .26 overall and d = .63 among those who noticed. Serial mediation (effort → parasocial connection → engagement) b = -0.13, 95% CI [-0.24, -0.05].
  - The effect is not explained by perceived quality, wariness of manipulation or general AI aversion. Disclosures that signal more effort shrink the penalty. — [JCR, doi 10.1093/jcr/ucag013](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/advance-article/doi/10.1093/jcr/ucag013/8672493); [SSIR summary](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ai-disclosures-social-media-disengagement); [Prolific citation listing (SSRN)](https://citations.prolific.com/paper/made-with-ai-consumer-engagement-with-social-media-containing-ai-disclosures)
  - A summary of the paper says a specific disclosure (naming Photoshop neural filters) raised perceived effort above a generic AI label, and the parasocial gap disappeared. — [Sherlock Comms summary](https://sherlockcomms.com/ai-content-labels-audience-reactions/)
- **[Peer-reviewed] Altay & Gilardi, *PNAS Nexus* 2024:** two preregistered experiments, N = 4,976 (US and UK). Labelling headlines "AI-generated" lowered perceived accuracy and willingness to share, whether the headlines were true or false and whether humans or AI wrote them. The penalty was about one third of the size of a "False" label. The proposed mechanism is that readers assume full AI automation with no human oversight. Outcomes were self-reported intentions. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11443540); [AllSides / press](https://www.allsides.com/news/2024-10-04-0915/technology-study-finds-people-are-skeptical-headlines-labeled-ai-generated); [Bluewin](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/people-distrust-ai-generated-headlines-2393402.html)
- **[Peer-reviewed] CHI 2025 (doi 10.1145/3706598.3713171), N = 911:** labels helped people identify AI-generated or altered content. The overall likelihood of liking, commenting or sharing did not change significantly (a null result). I could not access the paper (ACM returned a 403 error), so this comes from a secondary summary. — [Sherlock Comms](https://sherlockcomms.com/ai-content-labels-audience-reactions/); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713171)
- **[Peer-reviewed] *Electronic Markets* 2026 (doi 10.1007/s12525-026-00883-2):** two experiments (n = 325 and n = 371) on Instagram-style posts. "AI-generated" and "AI-enhanced" labels reduced affective and behavioural engagement compared with human-made content. The drop was largest for fully AI-generated content and for emotional rather than rational content. Late disclosure helped AI-enhanced content only. I did not get effect sizes (paywalled). — [Sherlock Comms](https://sherlockcomms.com/ai-content-labels-audience-reactions/); [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/ai-labels-can-reduce-social-media-engagement-study-finds/)
- **[Peer-reviewed] *Journal of Science Communication* 2025 (over 400 participants):** an AI disclosure on science posts lowered the credibility of *correct* information and raised the credibility of misinformation (a "truth-falsity crossover"). — [The Debrief](https://thedebrief.org/new-research-finds-ai-labels-can-backfire-making-misinformation-seem-more-credible/)
- **[Peer-reviewed] Marozzo et al. 2025, advertising (N = 230 and N = 324):** AI disclosure *raised* perceived credibility and engagement intentions, with credibility fully mediating the effect. This contrasts with the organic-content studies. — [IRIS UniMe](https://iris.unime.it/handle/11570/3344889)
- **[Peer-reviewed] *JMIS* 2024:** labelling news as AI-recommended reduced belief in true articles and had no material effect on belief in fake news. — [JMIS](https://jmis-web.org/articles/1672)
- **[Peer-reviewed/proceedings] HICSS (Univ. of Hawaii ScholarSpace), 787 TikToks from 30 creators, Nov 2024 to Apr 2025:** inauthenticity reduced liking and sharing, and the AI label *positively moderated* (softened) that negative relationship. Authors and coefficients were not retrieved (server unreachable). — [ScholarSpace](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/bitstreams/45602533-def1-431b-85f3-913c3e2ed312/download)
- **[Peer-reviewed] *Journal of Research in Interactive Marketing*:** existing followers were more resistant to the negative effect of AI disclosure than people with no prior relationship to the brand. — [Emerald JRIM](https://www.emerald.com/jrim/article-abstract/20/7/1017/1307938/Automated-interactions-authentic-bonds-AI?redirectedFrom=fulltext) (via [Sherlock Comms](https://sherlockcomms.com/ai-content-labels-audience-reactions/))
- **[Practitioner summary of an experiment] The Decision Lab (479 participants):** disclosure *increased* engagement but lowered perceived quality. — [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/the-science/this-video-was-made-by-ai)
- **[Survey] Attitudes:** in the US, 76% say telling AI from human content is extremely or very important, and 12% feel confident they can do so ([Pew, Sept 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)). Across 30 countries, 79% agree companies using AI should have to disclose it ([Ipsos AI Monitor 2025](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/conflicting-global-perceptions-around-ai-present-mixed-signals-brands)).

### Inferences
- For a small creator, the strongest field estimate is about 7% fewer likes per view on TikTok. That is a modest engagement cost. Because recommendation systems reward engagement rate, a small knock-on reach effect is plausible, though no study isolates it.
- The penalty works through perceived effort and the parasocial bond. Creator-written, specific disclosures that show effort (for example "script and drawings by Hoang, voice by AI") are the evidence-backed mitigation. Existing followers appear less sensitive than cold audiences.
- For educational or factual content the credibility findings matter more: AI labels tend to lower the perceived accuracy of *true* information (Altay & Gilardi; JSC 2025; JMIS 2024).

### Gaps
- I found no study that isolates the label's causal effect on *views or reach* (as opposed to engagement per view) on any platform.
- I found no peer-reviewed study specific to Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels or YouTube Shorts labels. The field data is TikTok only.
- I could not retrieve effect sizes for the *Electronic Markets*, JSC, CHI and HICSS papers.

## 3. Creator and agency A/B tests or case studies of AI-labelled versus unlabelled reels

### Takeaway
I found no credible controlled A/B test (same video, label on vs off) on any platform. What exists is correlational agency data and creator opinion. Treat it as weak evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **[Vendor/agency, correlational] Hallam agency analysis of 1,198 top TikToks:** AI videos averaged about 44.5k likes vs 69.4k for non-AI videos. This compares AI vs non-AI content, not label vs no label, and does not control for content type. Reported second-hand. — [GensoAI blog](https://gensoai.io/blog/do-ai-videos-get-less-views)
- **[Anecdotal] Newsletter commentary** argues that fear of reach loss from AI labels is mostly perception. No data. — [AI Social Media Simplified](https://ai-social-media-simplified.beehiiv.com/p/will-ai-labels-hurt-your-reach)
- **[Anecdotal] Creator/marketing blogs** (for example Jack Righteous, Storrito, Dataslayer) conclude that labelled posts are not penalised, mostly by citing TikTok's help text, not their own tests. — [Jack Righteous](https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/ai-art-visuals-creatives/tiktok-ai-music-video-labels-spam-reach-2026); [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/tiktok-ai-filter-how-it-kills-or-saves-creator-reach)
- **[Vendor, related but not about labels] Ravineo (44k Instagram creators):** found reach falling across the board, which it attributes to reel supply doubling while consumption stayed flat. This is a reminder that baseline reach is drifting down, which confounds informal before/after "label tests". — [Louderback / Inside the Creator](https://louderback.com/ai-is-a-harsh-mistress/)
- **[Platform data, unlabelled AI content still spreads]** Kapwing reported that more than half of posts recommended to new TikTok accounts are low-effort AI-generated videos, which suggests that AI content (labelled or not) is not systematically suppressed. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/14/tiktok-ai-transparency-tools-content-labels/amp/)

### Inferences
- A brand could run its own test, for example by alternating labelled and unlabelled posts of comparable format over several weeks. Small-account variance is large, though, so detecting a 7%-sized effect would need many posts. A test like that is unlikely to beat the JCR estimate.

### Gaps
- I found no published same-video label on/off experiment from any agency or creator. Marketing data firms (Later, Hootsuite, Sprout, Socialinsider, Buffer) did not turn up any label-specific dataset in my searches.

## 4. Audience reactions to AI or text-to-speech voiceovers vs human voices in explainer and educational videos

### Takeaway
For *learning* (comprehension and recall), modern high-quality AI voices perform about as well as human narration. Audio quality matters more than whether the voice is human or AI, and older robotic TTS hurt learning. For *trust and liking*, people still lean toward human voices, and a sizeable minority say AI voices in ads lower brand trust. Most of the trust data is vendor surveys. No rigorous study measures short-form *retention* (watch time) for AI vs human voiceover.

### Cited Findings
- **[Vendor experiment] TechSmith 2025:** identical training videos with different narrators. A clear AI voice beat muffled human audio, and "audio quality mattered far more than whether a voice was human or AI-generated". 92% said the high-quality AI voice felt professionally produced. Quiz scores rose with voice quality. Many could not tell a high-quality AI voice from a human one. English-speaking viewers preferred AI-voice disclosure more than German viewers did. TechSmith sells video tools. — [TechSmith](https://techsmith.com/blog/ai-voices-avatars-in-training-videos)
- **[Academic poster, science video] TUM:** understanding and memory stayed consistent across AI and human speakers, but viewers found content with visible speakers and natural human voices significantly more trustworthy than AI-generated content. — [TUM poster PDF](https://www.ja.tum.de/fileadmin/w00big/www/_my_direct_uploads/TUMJA_windfo_Poster_IV-v5.pdf)
- **[Conference paper] Eder & Hayashi, ICCE 2025:** natural voices were slightly preferred in lecture videos, but high-quality synthetic voices performed comparably on clarity and acceptability. — [APSCE ICCE library](https://library.apsce.net/index.php/ICCE/article/view/6068)
- **[Peer-reviewed] *Brno Studies in English* 2024:** learners gained knowledge equally from American-English and Czech-English TTS narration, but rated the American voice more competent. — [Brno Studies in English](https://journals.phil.muni.cz/bse/article/view/41144)
- **[Peer-reviewed] Liew et al. 2023, *Education and Information Technologies*:** modern TTS (Amazon Alexa) can convey enthusiasm and social cues suited to narrating multimedia lessons. — [Springer, doi 10.1007/s10639-022-11255-6](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/s10639-022-11255-6)
- **[Older peer-reviewed, context]** Studies from 2022 found students did not view AI voices negatively if they were "sufficiently human-like", and equal retention for human vs AI voice-overs with a convincing human-like presenter (Pi et al., 2022). The classic "voice principle" (Mayer) warned that mechanical TTS hurts learning, but that was based on older engines. — [Royal Roads MALAT review](https://malat-webspace.royalroads.ca/rru0277/ai-generated-audio-technology-in-learning/); [ReadSpeaker (vendor)](https://www.readspeaker.com/blog/elearning-voice)
- **[Vendor survey] Adobe Express (850 consumers, 205 marketers):** 88% of consumers have heard a voice and wondered if it was AI, 77% trust human voices most, and 58% would trust a brand less if it used AI voices in ads. Marketers in the same survey reported short-form videos with AI voice or music had the best engagement and ROI (self-report). — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/ai-audio-consumer-trust); [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/ai-voices-in-marketing-adobe/)
- **[Industry study, contested] Crowd React Media 2026 (radio):** the lead researcher called AI "technically competitive" in short-form voiceover. Industry readings of the study were divided. — [Barrett Media](https://barrettmedia.com/2026/07/14/crowd-react-media-study-divided-radio/)
- **[Unverified]** A voice-actor agency blog claims a December 2025 study found lower engagement for short video ads with AI vs human voiceover, strongest for emotional content. The blog does not name the study. — [Outspoken Voices](https://www.outspokenvoices.com/blog/ai-voice-or-human-voice-over-how-smart-brands-are-actually-using-both-in-2026)

### Inferences
- For a calm, factual business explainer with a high-quality AI voice (such as Kokoro), the learning-outcome evidence suggests little or no comprehension cost. The bigger risks are perceived warmth and authenticity, and the "low effort" inference that drives the JCR label penalty.
- Emotional content suffers more than rational content from AI disclosure (*Electronic Markets* 2026; Outspoken Voices claim). Educational explainers sit on the rational side, which likely shrinks the penalty.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study comparing watch time or completion rate for AI vs human voiceover in short-form social video.
- Most trust figures on AI voices are vendor surveys (Adobe, TechSmith) and should be weighted accordingly.
- I found no study of whether TTS that viewers recognise as such (for example TikTok's stock TTS voice, a native platform convention) carries the same trust penalty as a "realistic" AI voice.
