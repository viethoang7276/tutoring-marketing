# Text-message story / fake chat short-video format (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), 2024-2026

Scope note: about 16 searches and fetches, run on 27 Oct 2026. The platform-policy evidence is strong because it comes from primary pages plus mainstream tech press. The performance and best-practice evidence is weak: most of it comes from vendor blogs, and I found no peer-reviewed or platform-published retention data specific to chat-story videos. Claims are marked accordingly.

## 1. How does the format perform, and why does it retain viewers?

### Takeaway
At scale the format can pull very large view counts. The best documented case is a single app-marketing network that reports about 550M views. That evidence comes from one vendor-run analytics blog, though, and nobody has published retention data for chat-story videos as a genre. The retention mechanics that are claimed (cliffhangers, relatability, gameplay filler) are plausible but anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- RIZZ App (an AI dating-reply app) ran a network of 15+ TikTok accounts posting text-message stories over gameplay B-roll (often Minecraft). The reported totals are about 550M views and about 450K average views per video. Named accounts include @sentimental.texting, @normal.texts, @convoplug, @textjungle, @texttension, @textbix, @textcube, @textvox, @okaygesture and @dailytextingstoriess. Single vendor source, unverified. [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/rizz-apps-b-roll-strategy-that-got-them-half-a-billion-views-on-tiktok)
- Per-account figures in the same article: @textcube had 66 videos, about 1.4M average views and a 25% rate of videos passing 1M. @okaygesture had 76 videos, about 1.3M average and 9%. @textsrampage had 140 videos, about 850K average and 13%. Competitors Astra (@textiify, 150M+ views) and Plug AI (@textfun and @textswildin, about 126M combined) are also listed. Vendor source, unverified. [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/rizz-apps-b-roll-strategy-that-got-them-half-a-billion-views-on-tiktok)
- Those videos ran about **2 to 5 minutes**, much longer than typical 15-30 s Reels. The article credits retention to unfinished stories and cliffhangers (the curiosity gap), relatability ("texts mirror real conversations"), broad appeal from gaming visuals, and bite-sized length that encourages binge-watching. These explanations are anecdotal or editorial, not measured. [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/rizz-apps-b-roll-strategy-that-got-them-half-a-billion-views-on-tiktok)
- The gameplay layer belongs to the "sludge content" genre: split screens that pair the main content with repetitive mobile gameplay (notably Subway Surfers), ASMR or satisfying clips, "meant to increase viewer retention." Whether this helps or hurts attention is disputed in commentary, not settled by research. [Wikipedia: Sludge content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sludge_content); [YPulse 2023](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2023/04/28/tiktoks-sludge-content-is-the-result-of-gen-zs-wavering-attention-spans/)
- Tools have grown up around the format. texting.video takes a "Name: message" script and renders the video, with plans from $9/month. A $19.99 Gumroad guide sells the format as a way to earn followers and income. Both are evidence of commodification, not of performance. [AlternativeTo: texting.video](https://alternativeto.net/software/texting.video/about/); [Gumroad](https://dailystories.gumroad.com/l/e-book)
- A related screenshot-style trend, "texts with my situationship", shows the chat-screenshot aesthetic is mainstream on TikTok. [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/wellness/texts-with-my-situationship-tiktok-trend)

### Inferences
- **Curiosity gap:** each new bubble is a micro-reveal, so viewers keep watching to see the next reply. This is the same mechanism as cliffhanger storytelling. An accruals story should withhold the key reveal (for example, "but we haven't been paid yet") until late.
- **Reading pace:** reading is active, so viewers who are reading are not scrolling. If text appears faster than people can read, viewers rewind or loop, which can inflate watch time. If it appears too slowly, they drop. Section 3 gives a pacing benchmark taken from subtitling.
- **Sound cues:** notification "ding" or "whoosh" sounds mark each new message and pull attention back to the screen. I found no data measuring their effect.
- **Loopability:** ending on a punchline or end card that links back to the opening message encourages rewatches. This is anecdotal practice; I found no data.
- The headline view numbers come from drama and relationship stories pushed through multi-account networks. That is not evidence that an educational chat story with an end card would perform similarly.

### Gaps
- No platform-published or independent retention curves (average view duration, completion rate) for chat-story videos compared with other formats.
- No data on the effect of notification sounds, TTS voice or gameplay backgrounds on retention.
- No Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts-specific performance data for the format was found; the documented case is TikTok only.

## 2. Examples of education, finance, business, language-learning or study accounts using chat-style videos

### Takeaway
I found **no well-documented education, finance or business account** whose main format is chat stories and whose performance figures could be verified. Searches returned only general language-learning roundups and AI-video vendor pages. The documented successes are entertainment and app-marketing networks.

### Cited Findings
- Searches for chat-format accounting or finance explainers returned only HeyGen vendor landing pages for AI avatar explainers (accrual accounting, debits and credits, budgeting). None used the chat format. [HeyGen accounting](https://www.heygen.com/video/accounting-educational-video-maker)
- Language-learning roundups describe caption-led and meme or on-screen-text teaching. For example, @spanishteacher shows captions with translations and had about 21K followers per an older roundup, and Babbel notes meme accounts that teach "through context clues". These are text-overlay formats, not chat stories. [Lingopie](https://lingopie.com/blog/tiktok-accounts-to-learn-spanish/); [Babbel](https://www.babbel.com/learn-spanish/upper-intermediate/spanish-tiktoks)
- The closest brand or non-entertainment use of the sludge or split-screen aesthetic found was the 2024 Kamala Harris campaign, which tested "overstimulation" split-screen ads (main clip over Subway Surfers or soap-cutting footage). No performance data was visible behind the paywall. [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/kamala-harris-campaign-experiments-with-ads-for-an-audience-with-brain-rot/)
- The RIZZ network is the best documented case of a brand (an app) using chat stories for marketing, with a "subtle call to action" at the end. That is structurally close to Edlintics' story-then-end-card idea. [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/rizz-apps-b-roll-strategy-that-got-them-half-a-billion-views-on-tiktok)

### Inferences
- The lack of education examples cuts two ways. Edlintics has little direct competition in a "business chat story" niche, but there is also no proof that the format converts for learning content.
- The closest proven template is RIZZ's: a story carries the attention and the brand payoff comes at the end. The difference is that Edlintics' payoff, the concept definition, must feel earned by the story.

### Gaps
- No verified account names or view figures for finance, accounting, business or study accounts using chat stories. A manual TikTok search (for example "#textstory accounting", "group chat finance", "#studytok texts") in Chrome would be needed. The web search tools did not index TikTok well.
- I could not verify whether Duolingo or other ed-tech brands have run chat-format posts.

## 3. Best practices: length, pacing, font size, hooks, sound, captions, voice, end card

### Takeaway
I found no authoritative, data-backed best-practice guide specific to chat stories. Practical guidance has to be assembled from the documented app-network examples (long, cliffhanger-driven), general short-form hook advice, and subtitle reading-speed standards (about 17-20 characters per second for adults).

### Cited Findings
- **Length:** the high-view RIZZ-style videos run 2-5 minutes. Vendor advice for general short-form: 15 s for quick hooks, about 30 s for story arcs and layered jokes, about 5 s per scene in ads. [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/rizz-apps-b-roll-strategy-that-got-them-half-a-billion-views-on-tiktok); [Klap](https://klap.app/briefs/boost-your-tiktok-impact-master-text-overlay); [SundaySky](https://help.sundaysky.com/hc/en-us/articles/4420234841105)
- **Reading pace:** Netflix-derived subtitle standards cap English at about 17 cps (secondhand claim) or 20 cps (academic summary of the Netflix guide), with lines of at most 42 characters. Research cited by Middlebury says adults can handle 20 cps. Netflix's own marketing-asset guide sets a minimum on-screen time of 5/6 s per text event. [ProZ forum](https://mac.proz.com/forum/subtitling/369973-style_guide_of_subtitling.html); [CyberLeninka](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/techical-requirements-for-subtitles); [Middlebury](https://www.middlebury.edu/institute/advancing-your-career/career-guide/top-three-video-subtitling-mistakes-beginners-make); [Netflix supplementals guide](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000239632-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-Supplementals-Marketing-Assets)
- **Hooks:** a text hook in the first seconds "stops the scroll" and states the video's value, and on-screen text lets the hook land with the sound off. General advice, not specific to chat stories. [Skeepers](https://community.skeepers.io/blog/writing-text-hooks/); [Klap](https://klap.app/briefs/boost-your-tiktok-impact-master-text-overlay)
- **Sound:** cut on the soundtrack's beats. No source covered notification sounds specifically. [Klap](https://klap.app/briefs/boost-your-tiktok-impact-master-text-overlay)
- **Captions and voice (platform angle):** Meta's 2025 originality guidance tells creators to make captions high quality, which TechCrunch reads as a caution against unedited auto-captions. YouTube's policy excludes readings of material "you did not originally create". Marketing coverage says generic TTS over reused visuals is at risk, while TTS is acceptable with original scripts and human input. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content); [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [Newsgram](https://www.newsgram.com/science/2025/07/17/youtubes-monetisation-guidelines-july-15-2025-creators-need-know)

### Inferences (practical spec for Edlintics, derived from the above)
- **Length:** 25-45 s for an educational chat story with an end card. Use the 2-5 minute length only for drama-led serial stories, which do not fit a definition-led lesson.
- **Pacing:** about 17 cps means a 50-character message needs about 3 s on screen, and no message should stay under about 0.8 s (Netflix minimum). In practice that is roughly 1 message every 1.5-3 s, or 10-18 messages in 30-40 s. Add a "typing…" indicator before the punchline messages to build suspense.
- **Font size:** bubbles must stay readable on a phone. Keep text inside the platform safe zones (avoid the bottom roughly 20% and the right-side button rail) and avoid long bubbles. Wrap lines at about 30-42 characters.
- **Hook:** the first message should be the conflict, for example "Why does the P&L say we made $12,000 when the bank has $800?". Showing the group chat name on screen ("Month-end close 😩") also sets context instantly.
- **Sound:** one soft notification tick per message plus low music. This matches the existing house rule of a music track at volume 8. A voice is optional: a Kokoro voice reading the messages adds the "narration and commentary" value that YouTube's policy text favours. A character-per-sender voice setup is more work.
- **End card:** hold it 3-5 s with the term, a one-sentence definition, and a link back to the opening line so the video loops. Put the definition in the voice track too, so it counts as educational value rather than "scrolling text with minimal narrative."
- **Gameplay background:** avoid it. It adds sludge or brainrot signals (section 4) and creates third-party footage reuse risk under the reused-content rules (section 5). Use a branded background or a simple office illustration instead (for example Ledger).

### Gaps
- No A/B data on TTS compared with silent, sound cues compared with none, or gameplay compared with plain backgrounds for chat videos.
- No platform-official font-size spec; the safe-zone percentages above are common practice, not sourced here.

## 4. Is the format saturated or "brainrot"-coded, and is there a brand risk?

### Takeaway
Yes, partly. In 2024-2026 the gameplay-backed fake-text story sits alongside Reddit-story TTS and split-screen sludge as a recognised low-effort or "brainrot" genre. It is mass-produced by multi-account networks and sold as a template through tools and courses. The chat interface itself is neutral; the brainrot signal comes from gameplay backgrounds, robotic TTS and clickbait drama.

### Cited Findings
- Sludge or split-screen content with Subway Surfers gameplay is widely discussed as attention-bait and linked to "brain rot" in commentary. [Wikipedia: Sludge content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sludge_content); [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2023/04/28/tiktoks-sludge-content-is-the-result-of-gen-zs-wavering-attention-spans/)
- One app company runs 15+ near-identical text-story accounts and competitors copy the blueprint. The source itself flags "content fatigue … copycat saturation" as a risk. [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/rizz-apps-b-roll-strategy-that-got-them-half-a-billion-views-on-tiktok)
- The format is sold as a cheap template ($9/month generators, $19.99 "make money" guides). [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/texting.video/about/); [Gumroad](https://dailystories.gumroad.com/l/e-book)
- A presidential campaign borrowed the aesthetic specifically to target an audience "with brain rot", which confirms the cultural coding. [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/kamala-harris-campaign-experiments-with-ads-for-an-audience-with-brain-rot/)

### Inferences
- For a "calm, expert" learning brand whose audience includes business professionals, the gameplay and robotic-TTS version is off-brand. A clean, branded chat UI with a realistic workplace scenario reads as a sketch or a meme, much like the office comics Edlintics already posts, not as sludge.
- Brand risk rises with volume and sameness. A handful of distinct, well-written stories is fine; a daily stream of near-identical templates looks like a content farm to both humans and platform classifiers (section 5).

### Gaps
- No survey or brand-lift data on how audiences perceive brands that use chat-story videos.

## 5. Platform-policy risks of producing the format at scale

### Takeaway
None of the three platforms bans chat-style videos as such. All three penalise **unoriginal or reused material** and, especially on YouTube, **templated, mass-produced or repetitive** output. YouTube's policy wording, "templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value", is the closest match to a careless chat-story pipeline. The penalties are demonetisation (YouTube, Facebook) and reduced distribution or For You ineligibility (TikTok, Meta), not removal. Original scripts, distinct concepts per video and real educational value are the safe harbour.

### Cited Findings

**TikTok**
- "Content is also ineligible for the FYF if it includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new." Also: "You should only post content you created or have the right to share." [TikTok Community Guidelines: Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- TikTok "strictly prohibit[s] automation tools, scripts, or other tricks designed to bypass our systems". This covers mass-posting automation, not templated editing. [TikTok CG](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- The FYF standards page says ineligible categories are listed in "FYF Ineligible" sections across the guidelines and that low views don't necessarily mean a rule was broken. [TikTok FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)
- Third-party summary: repeated FYF-ineligible posting can make a whole account temporarily ineligible and harder to find in search, with notification and appeal. Reposts carrying other platforms' watermarks are flagged. Secondary source. [Online Optimism](https://onlineoptimism.com/blog/updates-tiktoks-community-guidelines); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/tiktok-ads/tiktok-algorithm-for-you-feed/)
- I found no specific TikTok wording on "templated" or "mass-produced" originals. TikTok's test is "reused material without anything new", so original scripts in a repeated template appear to be safe.

**YouTube**
- On 2 July 2025 YouTube announced a YPP update effective 15 July 2025 that renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" to better catch "mass-produced and repetitious" content. Creator liaison Rene Ritchie called it a minor update, since such content was already ineligible. [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)
- Policy text (still headed "Generic or Repetitive Content"): it covers content "that looks like it's made with a template". Not allowed: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"; "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". Allowed: the "same intro and outro … but the bulk of your content is different"; a series where "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept." [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Reused content: allowed "if viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference". Not allowed: "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create"; content copied "without any substantive modifications". [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- This is a **monetisation** policy, so videos can still be uploaded. Marketing coverage says TTS-narrated Reddit-story-style channels with near-identical narration are prime targets. [Newsgram](https://www.newsgram.com/science/2025/07/17/youtubes-monetisation-guidelines-july-15-2025-creators-need-know); [subsub.io](https://www.subsub.io/blog/inauthentic-content-why-so-many-creators-are-getting-demonetized)
- One vendor site claims a further restructuring in July 2026 that split inauthentic content into three sections. **Unverified**; I could not confirm it from YouTube. [easyviral.ai](https://easyviral.ai/api/blog-md/can-you-monetize-ai-generated-videos-on-youtube)

**Meta (Facebook and Instagram)**
- On 14 July 2025 Meta announced a crackdown on accounts that repeatedly repost others' text, photos or video. Penalties are loss of monetisation for a period and reduced distribution across the account. Duplicate videos are downranked in favour of the original, and links to originals are being tested. Allowed: reactions, trend participation and added commentary. Guidance: don't just stitch clips or add watermarks, focus on "authentic storytelling" over low-value short videos, and use high-quality captions. The rollout was gradual over the following months. Meta also removed about 10M impersonator profiles in H1 2025. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content); [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/facebooks-crackdown-on-reposted-content-what-creators-need-to-know); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250715-meta-combating-unoriginal-facebook-content)
- Creators can check distribution and penalty risk in the Professional Dashboard and on the Support home. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content)
- Instagram: since April 2024 the algorithm has favoured originals, replaced recommended reposts with originals, labelled reposts and removed aggregator posts from recommendations. Coverage from April and May 2026 reports further originality enforcement. In the newsletter's account of Mosseri, adding your own voiceover, text or commentary qualifies as original. The 2026 details are secondary only; PetaPixel's article returned 403. [Gigazine 2024](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240502-instagram-updated-algorithm-prioritizes-original-content); [PetaPixel 2026 (search snippet)](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [Post Planner newsletter via Milled](https://milled.com/postplanner/igs-new-30-day-rule-will-kill-50-of-accounts-ejL99Dh0UIX5mqVE)

### Inferences: does templated chat video fall under these rules, and how to stay safe?
- **Original scripts in a consistent visual template are not "reused" content** on any platform. The real exposure is YouTube's "templated storylines / scrolling text with minimal … educational value" and "same situation, same outcome" clauses, plus classifier-level sameness on TikTok and Meta if dozens of near-identical videos ship.
- The **gameplay background is the clearest reused-content risk.** It is usually third-party footage, which brings Meta duplicate detection and TikTok "reused material" exposure. Drop it.
- Safe practices:
  1. Give each video a distinct scenario and concept (YouTube's "distinct storyline, focus, or concept" test).
  2. Add narration or commentary: a voice line or an on-screen explanation that teaches, not just bubbles.
  3. End on a real educational payoff, the definition end card.
  4. Vary characters, settings and outcomes across the series.
  5. Use only owned, licensed or platform-library audio and visuals; no watermarks from other apps.
  6. Pace output: a few strong chat stories per week mixed with other formats, not a daily template flood.
  7. Don't use multi-account networks or posting automation (TikTok prohibits automation to bypass systems).
  8. Disclose AI where required. This is the subject of the separate AI-labels research.
- Monetisation exposure is low for Edlintics today, since it is not in YPP or Facebook monetisation as far as I know. The relevant penalty is **distribution** (FYF ineligibility, Meta downranking), which matters more for an organic-growth brand.

### Gaps
- TikTok publishes no threshold for "anything new"; enforcement is opaque.
- No reported case of a creator penalised specifically for original-script chat-story videos (as opposed to Reddit-TTS or reupload channels) was found.
- Instagram's 2026 originality changes could not be read in a primary source (PetaPixel 403; no Instagram blog fetched).
