## TL;DR — the five things that matter

1. **We are 12 weeks from launch and the clock is the strategy.** IE classes start **Aug 31, 2026**; we launch **Sept 1**. Midterm panic for our persona peaks **mid-to-late October into early November**; finals **early–mid December**. Evergreen content takes weeks to rank. **So the back-catalogue has to be built in June–August to harvest the October panic** — if we wait until October to post, we show up after the buying intent has passed.

2. **Do not chase "fintok."** 76% of Gen Z learn finance from TikTok/YouTube — but that's **personal** finance (budgeting, investing, "get rich"). Our buyer is the opposite intent: an anxious undergrad who needs to pass **Financial Accounting** — T-accounts, consolidation, deferred tax, DCF. Same platforms, different universe. Chasing fintok topics buys views from people who will never pay €99. Our lane is **exam-concept content**, thin and deep, not money-influencer content.

3. **The native format is the moat on screen.** Our edge is "you drive, I interrupt" — a worked problem solved live. The content version of that is a **screen-recording of Hoang solving an exam-style problem in Excel/Sheets**, narrated calmly. Not talking-head tips. The format *is* a demo of the product. This is the single highest-leverage thing we can make, and almost nobody in academic finance does it well.

4. **YouTube is our spine; TikTok/Reels are the net.** YouTube = search intent ("how to consolidate financial statements") + evergreen ranking + a calm, long-ish voice that matches the brand. TikTok/Reels = serendipity, reaching Sofía *before* she's searching. Lead with YouTube (Shorts + a few long worked-examples), repurpose down to TikTok/Reels. The brand's no-hype, no-emoji voice fits YouTube far better than TikTok's hype culture — a real argument for where to plant the flag.

5. **AI is the backdrop, not a side note.** Students already use ChatGPT to study — but on accounting assessments students score ~77% vs ChatGPT's ~47%. The honest, ownable message: *AI gives you an answer; it can't watch you try and catch the moment your reasoning breaks.* That's exactly our product. AI raises, not lowers, the value of "someone watched you do it."

## 1. The seasonal spine (the backbone of everything)

Our audience does not buy on a flat line. Demand for exam help is violently seasonal, and the European autumn cycle is the calendar we live on. Anchored to **IE University 2026-2027** (our persona's school; full PDF dates below), generalised to the EU prestige cohort.

### IE University 2026-2027 — fall term key dates
| Date | Event | Marketing meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Aug 31** | Classes start (all students) | Term-start awareness window opens |
| Sep 1–2 | First-year activities | Freshers arriving, syllabi handed out |
| Oct 12 | Spanish holiday | — |
| Oct 26 | Segovia holiday | (IE Segovia campus) |
| **~Oct (wks 6–8)** | Midterms (continuous assessment — no single "exam week", they cluster here) | **Primary conversion window for course #1** |
| Nov 2 | Spanish holiday | — |
| Nov 9 | Madrid holiday | — |
| **early Nov** | Midterm wave continues (Sofía's Nov Financial Accounting midterm) | **Peak panic** |
| **Dec 18** | End of classes → finals cluster the weeks before | **Second conversion wave (finals)** |
| Dec 19 – Jan 10 | Winter break | Dead air for studying; plan/reflect content only |
| **Jan 11** | Second semester starts | Cycle repeats for spring |

> IE uses **continuous assessment**, so there is no single stamped "midterm week" — midterms are scheduled inside each course, typically weeks 6–8, i.e. **mid-to-late October**. This matches `product.md`'s persona exactly (Sofía's FA midterm in November). LSE/LBS run a three-term system with a reading week mid-Michaelmas (early Nov) and heavier exams in winter/summer; HEC/ESCP/Bocconi cluster autumn exams Dec–Jan. **Net: the EU cohort's autumn pain is Oct → mid-Dec, with an Oct–early-Nov midterm spike and a Dec finals spike.**

### The demand curve, in one line
**June–Aug:** build (no buyers yet, but this is when content must be planted). → **Sept:** awareness, low panic, high openness (new syllabi). → **mid-Oct–early-Nov:** midterm spike, highest buying intent. → **late-Nov–mid-Dec:** finals spike. → **late-Dec–early-Jan:** dead air. → **mid-Jan:** spring cycle restarts.

## 2. How our actual buyer consumes (and where to reach her)

Grounded in `product.md` (Sofía) + live data:

- **Short-form is the default.** Gen Z consumes in small chunks; dislikes videos > ~10 min (Sofía specifically, per `product.md`); 76% turn to TikTok/YouTube/Reddit for finance learning.
- **But intent splits by platform.** TikTok/Reels = passive discovery (she's scrolling, not looking for us). YouTube = active search (she's stuck on a problem at 11pm and types it in). **YouTube captures the high-intent moment; TikTok/Reels create the earlier awareness.**
- **She's skeptical.** 82% of Gen Z know social finance advice oversimplifies. Our calm, precise, no-hype voice is an *asset* against that fatigue — it reads as the adult in the room. This is a brand-fit advantage, not just a constraint.
- **Reddit is an unscanned channel.** r/accounting, university subreddits, course-specific Discords are where panicked students actually ask "how do I do X for the exam." Worth a low-effort presence (answer, don't sell). Flagging for the channel decision.

## 3. Platform & format mechanics (2026 state of play)

What actually works right now, per current best-practice data:

- **The first 3 seconds decide everything** — ~80% of completion variance. Open mid-action (the journal entry already half-written), never "hi, today we'll learn…".
- **Completion > length.** A 45s video at 70% completion beats a 15s at 40%. We can afford to actually teach a concept, not just tease it.
- **Platform signals differ:**
  - **YouTube Shorts:** rewards viewer satisfaction + staying in-app + *returning*. Favours genuinely useful content (our strength).
  - **TikTok:** **saves are 2–3× likes** in value. "Save this for your exam" is the natural, on-brand CTA — no hype required.
  - **Reels:** watch-time, likes/reach, **sends/reach** (DM shares). "Send to your study group" is the lever.
- **Cadence:** TikTok/Reels 3–5×/week; YouTube Shorts 2–3×/week is enough. Realistically, for one operator pre-launch, **2–3 high-quality pieces/week, repurposed across platforms**, beats spreading thin.
- **Don't just clone across platforms** — same core asset, re-cut per platform (caption density, pace, CTA).

## 4. The competitive / creator landscape — and the gap we own

- **Personal-finance creators are saturated** (Humphrey Yang, Mr Money Jar, etc. — 100M+ monthly views combined). Not our space.
- **Academic accounting educators exist but are generic & global:** "Accounting Stuff" (debits/credits basics), Professor Farhat (GAAP/US-CPA depth). They teach *concepts in the abstract* for a *global/US* audience.
- **The open gap:** **exam-targeted, EU-business-school-flavoured, worked-problem content** in a calm documentation voice, by a named operator who'll then get on a call with you. Nobody owns "the person who solves *your* kind of exam problem on screen and then watches you try." That's defensible and it's exactly what the product is.

**Strategic warning (not a yes-man):** the topic-led + trademark rule (lead with the *concept*, university only as secondary context, never the headline) **costs us the single highest-intent hook** — "IE Financial Accounting midterm." That phrase is what Sofía would actually search/click. We're choosing legal safety + independence over click-through. I agree with the call, but we should go in eyes-open: we win the *concept* search ("how to do consolidation") and let the school live in the description/secondary line, accepting we forfeit some branded-exam intent. Worth a deliberate decision tomorrow, not a default.

## 5. The AI dynamic

- Students already lean on ChatGPT for study; risk of over-reliance + shallow understanding is now a documented concern.
- On **accounting** assessments specifically, students (~77%) still beat ChatGPT (~47%) — accounting is a domain where AI is unreliable enough to be dangerous to lean on.
- **Ownable message:** *"AI will hand you an answer. It can't watch you solve it and stop you at the exact moment your logic breaks. That's what the session is."* This reframes AI from competitor to setup for our moat — and it's true, not spin. A content pillar in itself ("what ChatGPT gets wrong about [concept]").

## 6. Proposed directions to argue tomorrow (3)

Each is a candidate **pillar/campaign** — not locked. We'll reshape with your judgement and then write the survivors into `calendar/calendar.json`.

### Direction A — "Worked, not watched" (the format-led flagship)
Screen-recorded, calm, Excel/Sheets walk-throughs of one exam-style problem each (T-accounts, a cash-flow statement, a consolidation, a CVP break-even, deferred tax). The content **is** the moat demo. YouTube long-ish (5–8 min) as the evergreen anchor, cut into Shorts/TikTok/Reels hooks ("the one consolidation mistake that costs you the marks").
- **Evidence:** completion>length favours real teaching; YouTube rewards usefulness + search intent; format directly previews the paid session; no competitor owns it.
- **What to film:** 5 cornerstone problems mapped to the 5 launch courses. Build in June–July so they rank by October.

### Direction B — "The concept you'll be tested on" (seasonal SEO harvest)
A concept-per-week content calendar **reverse-engineered from the autumn exam cycle**, front-loaded so each concept is already ranking when its exam arrives. October = the accounting-midterm concepts; December = finals concepts.
- **Evidence:** demand is violently seasonal; ranking has lag; this is the mechanism that converts the seasonal spine into pipeline.
- **What to film:** a dated production schedule working backward from Oct/Dec, concept list pulled from the 5 course syllabi.

### Direction C — "What AI gets wrong" (differentiator pillar)
A recurring series taking a concept where ChatGPT confidently misleads, showing the wrong answer, then the right reasoning — closing on "this is why someone watches you try."
- **Evidence:** AI is top-of-mind for students; accounting is where AI is measurably weak; reframes our moat; novel angle few finance creators run credibly.
- **What to film:** 3–4 "AI gets this wrong" problems to start; cheap to produce alongside A.

My instinct: **A is the spine, B is the schedule that A runs on, C is the sharpest single differentiator.** They're complementary, not competing — the real question tomorrow is sequencing and how many we can sustain as one operator pre-launch.

## 7. Scenario planning (as requested — different scenarios)

| Scenario | Trigger | Play |
|---|---|---|
| **Base case** | Build June–Aug, launch Sept 1 | Plant evergreen worked-examples now; ramp posting into the Oct midterm spike; convert on finals in Dec. |
| **Late start** | Content build slips into Aug/Sept | Triage to **Direction B's October-critical concepts only** (accounting midterm set); skip breadth; accept a thinner net. Don't spread across 5 courses — pick the 1–2 that convert. |
| **A format wins early** | A worked-example over-performs pre-launch | Double down on that concept's *course*, make it the launch hero, reorder the build queue around proven demand. |
| **Nothing lands by launch** | Low reach through September | Shift from organic-only to **intent capture**: YouTube SEO + Reddit/Discord helpfulness in course-specific communities, where intent is explicit and reach isn't algorithm-gated. |
| **Cannibalization watch** (`vision.md`) | 1:1 students shift to €99 course but no net-new buyers by ~4 wks post-launch | Marketing's tell that the *audience/message* is off, not just the funnel — re-test the concept-led hook vs. a sharper pain hook before scaling spend/effort. |
| **Spring repeat** | Jan 11 term restart | Re-run the autumn playbook on the spring cycle with the now-ranked back-catalogue compounding. |
