- **The hooks half is solid, the "what is working now" half is thin.** Public web fetches of TikTok, Instagram and YouTube pages do not expose post-level URLs or like/view counts without a login. ### Our version for each hook

- **Jobs report, Fri 6 Nov (puzzle, 10:00 slot).** Fictional Riverton has 1,000 adults: 600 are employed, 40 are looking for work and 360 are not looking. What is the unemployment rate? Answer 40 / 640 = 6.25%, and the participation rate is 640 / 1,000 = 64%. Hook: "Riverton has 1,000 adults and only 40 are unemployed. So why is the rate not 4%?". Teaches that the denominator is the labour force.
- **BoE and rates, Thu 5 Nov (definition reel, 18:00).** A $1,000 bond pays $40 a year. New bonds now pay 5%. Simplified, as a perpetuity, the old bond is worth $40 / 0.05 = $800. Hook: "When rates rise, the bond you already own gets cheaper". State the perpetuity assumption on the slide.
- **Apple earnings, Mon 2 Nov (explainer or carousel).** Use a fictional chain, not Apple: revenue rises from $2.0 million to $2.2 million (up 10%) while costs rise from $1.8 million to $2.1 million, so profit falls from $200,000 to $100,000. Hook: "Sales rose 10% and profit fell 50%. Both are true". Do not use Apple's numbers or logo.
- **Trade balance, Wed 4 Nov (This vs That carousel).** Trade deficit versus national debt, with a fictional country exporting $50 billion and importing $80 billion. Hook: "A trade deficit is not the same as debt".
- **Tariffs as a tax (carousel, Tue 3 Nov, only if cleared).** A fictional importer pays a 10% duty on a $100 shipment, so landed cost is $110. Who ends up paying depends on prices. Hook: "A tariff is paid at the port. Who bears it is a separate question".
- **ACCA and CFA windows (comic, Fri or Sun).** A comic about the week before an exam, ending with THE RULE on a concept like accrual versus cash. Mention no exam body in the visuals; the independence rule applies.
- **Black Friday build-up (meme comic or five-concepts, from 8 Nov, ahead of 27 Nov).** Five concepts of retail season: markup, margin, inventory turnover, working capital, break-even. Hook: "A 20% discount does not cost you 20% of profit". Worked in section 4, script 3.

## 4. First chat-story reel scripts

Format assumed: voice-free, fictional Slack-style office chat, about 10-14 messages, one concept each, a final line stating the rule on screen. Names are invented. Figures are checked below each script. House rules: USD, no exclamation points, no emoji, no full stop ending the on-screen final line. The evidence that this format works in finance education is not established (see 1.7), so label the first one as a test with a one-line hypothesis, as the weekly process requires for idea tests.

### Script 1. "The $12,000 that is not revenue yet" (accrual versus cash)
Channel: #finance-close
- Maya (Sales): Atlas Corp just signed. They paid $12,000 today for a 12-month plan starting 1 Nov
- Maya: Booking $12,000 in revenue for October, right
- Dev (Accounting): Not quite
- Maya: The money is in the bank
- Dev: Cash is in. Revenue is earned as we deliver, so $1,000 a month
- Maya: So October revenue is...
- Dev: $0. November gets the first $1,000
- Maya: And the other $11,000
- Dev: Sits on the balance sheet as deferred revenue, a liability, until we earn it
- On screen: Cash arrives when it is paid. Revenue arrives when it is earned
Check: $12,000 / 12 = $1,000. After one month, $11,000 deferred. Correct under straight-line recognition.

### Script 2. "Where did the laptop go?" (depreciation)
Channel: #office-ops
- Priya (Ops): I just bought a $1,800 laptop for Sam
- Tom (Finance): Noted. It will not hit the P&L as $1,800 this month
- Priya: Why not, I paid it
- Tom: It lasts about 3 years, so we spread the cost: $1,800 / 36 months
- Priya: That is $50 a month
- Tom: Right. The asset shrinks on the balance sheet, and $50 hits the P&L each month
- Priya: And cash
- Tom: Cash left on day one. That is the difference
- On screen: Depreciation spreads the cost over the years the asset helps earn
Check: 1,800 / 36 = 50. Assumes straight-line, 3 years, zero salvage; state it.

### Script 3. "The 20% discount" (margin and break-even volume)
Channel: #sales-pricing
- Lena (Sales): Black Friday plan: 20% off the $40 backpack
- Omar (Finance): Unit cost is $30. What is the profit per unit now
- Lena: $10, so we lose 20% of it, $2
- Omar: Check again. 20% off $40 is $8, so the price is $32
- Lena: Profit is $2 a unit
- Omar: $10 down to $2. That is an 80% drop per unit
- Lena: How many extra sales do we need
- Omar: Five times as many to earn the same total profit
- On screen: A discount comes out of margin, not out of price
Check: 40 x 0.8 = 32; 32 - 30 = 2; 10 / 2 = 5x. Correct. Lena's first line of arithmetic is intentionally wrong and corrected, so the on-screen reveal must show the correction clearly.

### Script 4. "Profit on paper, empty bank" (working capital and cash gap)
Channel: #supply-chain
- Ravi (Ops): I ordered 2,000 jackets for the holiday rush at $15 each
- Ana (Finance): That is $30,000 cash out in October
- Ravi: We sell them at $30 in late November, so $60,000
- Ana: Profit is $30,000, agreed. Our supplier wants payment now, customers pay in 30 days
- Ravi: So we are fine
- Ana: Profit is fine. Cash is short until January
- Ravi: Profit and cash are not the same
- On screen: A growing business can run out of cash while it is profitable
Check: 2,000 x $15 = $30,000; 2,000 x $30 = $60,000; profit $30,000 ignoring other costs. State "ignoring other costs" on screen.

### Script 5. "The $80,000 meeting" (sunk cost)
Channel: #product
- Kai (Product): We have spent $80,000 on the booking feature
- Kai: Finishing it costs $20,000 more and should bring in $15,000
- Zoe (Finance): Then the $80,000 is not part of the choice
- Kai: But we cannot waste it
- Zoe: It is gone either way. Today the choice is spend $20,000 to get $15,000
- Kai: That loses $5,000
- Zoe: Yes. Stop, or find a cheaper way to finish
- On screen: Only future costs and future benefits belong in the decision
Check: 15,000 - 20,000 = -5,000. Correct. Zoe adds the cheaper-path option so the rule does not read as "always quit".
