# Brand demo ads: how learning apps explain themselves, and where Edlintics fits

Date: 2026-11-18. Author: market researcher. Status: evidence for the strategy session, not a decision.

## TL;DR

- I could not watch or read the three reference videos (YouTube page text and the iSpot page were not retrievable: iSpot returned 403, YouTube returned navigation only). Section 1 therefore rests on Brilliant's and Duolingo's own pages plus third-party commentary. Someone should watch the three videos and log shot-by-shot structure before the storyboard is locked.
- Brilliant's current homepage positions around a "visual, interactive tutor" for math and coding. Its course list has no finance, business or economics (checked 2026-11-18). That lane is open on the Brilliant side.
- The finance-training competitors (CFI, Wall Street Prep) sell job certification for investment banking and corporate finance. None of the pages read claims interactive, bite-sized fundamentals for undergraduates.
- Meta's own guidance supports a sound-on, vertical, safe-zone-respecting demo with the hook inside the first few seconds. Caption guidance from Meta was not found.
- Recommended direction to test: show the real card UI answering a real exam-type question, topic first, with one plain "who we are" line at the end.

## 1. How Brilliant and Duolingo explain themselves

What I could verify:

- Brilliant homepage (fetched 2026-11-18): headline "Your personal tutor for math and coding"; subhead about a "visual, interactive tutor"; line "A visual, interactive tutor, not a cheating machine"; proof as "100,000+ five-star app reviews" and "Trusted by more than 5,000 schools"; CTA "Get started". Source: https://brilliant.org
- Brilliant's App Store title is "Brilliant: Learn by doing". Source: https://apps.apple.com/ec/app/brilliant-learn-by-doing/id913335252
- Ad-library aggregators list the recurring themes as "learn by doing, not watching", interactive problem-solving and "15 minutes a day"; they also state that new course launches drive paid social. These are third-party summaries of unclear method. Source: https://adlibrary.com/brands/brilliant
- An ad tracker reports 101 Meta ads from Brilliant between 18 Oct and 12 Nov 2026, all video. Third-party figure, not verified against Meta Ad Library. Source: https://admakeai.com/advertisers/brilliant-org
- Duolingo: coverage describes a social voice built on serialized, humorous, mascot-led content ("treat socials like a sitcom"), and in-feed video ads of about 15 seconds. Secondary sources only. Sources: https://technical.ly/company-culture/duolingo-viral-marketing-strategy-lessons and https://www.latterly.org/duolingo-marketing-strategy/
- Duolingo chess: announced April 2025, with a stated marketing view that chess is seen as "overly intellectual or inaccessible". Source: https://sportsdigest.in/duolingo-chess-campaign-features-pragg/176245/amp/ and https://blog.duolingo.com/chess-course. I found no source confirming a 2021 "Duocon: Coming Soon" video, and the "Learn Chess on Duolingo" video page returned no details (length 25 s is the brief's figure, unverified).

What I could not verify: shot structure, pacing, UI-versus-animation split and people-versus-no-people for the three named videos. Reasonable hypothesis for the strategy session to confirm by viewing: product on a phone, interaction close-ups, one promise line, a store CTA. Treat that as a hypothesis, not a finding.

Pattern worth borrowing without copying: both brands say what the learner does ("learn by doing", "bite-sized", play-like) rather than what the product contains, and both lean on one repeated positioning phrase.

## 4. What makes a product-demo short work on Reels

- Hook early: Instagram's creator guidance says the first three seconds are critical. Source: https://creators.instagram.com/best-practices?locale=en_US
- Sound: Meta's developer blog says over 75% of Reels views are sound on, and reports higher positive response for ads with both music and voiceover (Meta's own research, 2024). Source: https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/11/07/unlock-the-power-of-reel-ads/
- Format and safe zone: 9:16, key text and calls to action away from the edges; for image ads Meta suggests keeping roughly 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side clear. This is an image-ad spec, so apply as a conservative guide to video. Sources: https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads and https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/image/instagram-reels
- Vendor blogs (lower authority): show the product in action or the problem in the first seconds, not a logo; label the result on screen; a hook of roughly 5 to 10 words held 1 to 3 seconds; keep demos under about 90 seconds. Sources: https://quso.ai/blog/instagram-reels-best-practices, https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/instagram-reels-hooks-two-step-test, https://coinis.com/how-to/product-reel-for-instagram. These are rules of thumb, not platform data.
- UI legibility and caption best practice: no authoritative source found. Our own rule (captions mid-bottom, no text spill over boxes) already governs; Meta's safe-zone figures above argue for testing the bottom third carefully.

Consistency check against house memory: an earlier internal rule says a hook is fine when it matches what our top Instagram reels did, and direct title opening is the default. A demo that opens on the real card with its question fits both.

## Implications for Edlintics

- Open on a real glossary or Check yourself card with the question visible and legible, then show the tap and the answer. Topic first, brand line last.
- Use real UI, not animation, for the demo format. That is the point of "who we are"; Ledger can frame it but not replace it. This is a creative call for the strategy session.
- Keep the promise line to one repeated phrase, as Brilliant does, and test one of the section 3 candidates.
- Do not claim learner numbers or outcomes; none exist yet pre-launch.
