# Copyright-safe Christmas music for December 2026 short videos

Dated 31 Oct 2026. Author: market researcher. Status: evidence for the CMO, not a decision. Not legal advice.

Scope: Christmas background music for Edlintics organic short videos (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, Threads), built locally and finished in Canva at volume 8 under a voiceover. Edlintics is a commercial business, so non-commercial (NC) licences are out.

## TL;DR

- Safe to use: a recording that carries its own commercial licence, of a public-domain composition. The composition being old does not clear the recording.
- Best verified route today: Pixabay "Christmas" solo-piano tracks (Pixabay Content License, no attribution, commercial use allowed). Catch: the track pages are labelled "Content ID Registered", so keep the licence proof and expect to dispute a possible automated claim.
- Canva stock music marked "Commercial use allowed" is the stated-safe default. Canva "Popular Music" is personal use only and must not be used for Edlintics. I could not read Canva's own policy pages (HTTP 403), so the Canva claims below rest on search-result summaries of Canva's pages and need a manual check in the Canva UI.
- TikTok's Commercial Music Library is reportedly cleared for use inside TikTok only. Do not export a CML-based video and post it on the other six platforms.
- Avoid: any modern song (All I Want for Christmas, Last Christmas, etc.) and the Wilhousky 1936 "Carol of the Bells" lyrics or arrangement.

## 1. Composition versus recording

Two separate rights exist in every piece of music. The composition (melody and lyrics) and the sound recording (one specific performance). A public-domain composition can be freely re-performed. A recording of it is a new work owned by the performer or label and needs its own licence.

Source for the general principle and the warning that a public-domain melody does not cover a modern recording or arrangement: https://sonilo.com/ai-music/free-christmas-music (third-party guide, low authority).

Carol of the Bells guidance: use a plain Leontovych-melody instrumental only, never the English lyrics. Most "Carol of the Bells" piano or orchestral versions follow the Wilhousky arrangement's harmony, which is the protected part. My own date arithmetic, unverified: a US work published in 1936 should enter the public domain on 1 Jan 2032. Treat the track in section 3 as a lower-priority pick for this reason.

Not safe: All I Want for Christmas Is You, Last Christmas, and any other modern song. Cover versions do not help, because the composition is still copyrighted and the cover recording needs a sync licence. Also named as copyrighted by CD Baby: Santa Claus Is Coming to Town, A Holly Jolly Christmas, Do You Hear What I Hear, Frosty the Snowman.

### (b) Recordings: what makes one safe

A recording is safe when its owner has granted a commercial licence that covers social media use, and you can show that licence. Famous orchestral recordings of the Nutcracker (label-owned, even if Tchaikovsky is in the public domain) are not safe. Musopen-style recordings made for release into the public domain are the exception (section 2).

## 4. Risks

- Content ID false claims: Pixabay tracks are Content ID registered. Keep the Pixabay track URL and a dated screenshot of the licence line per video. Use the same track in only a few videos, since repeated use across accounts raises the chance of an automated match. Canva documents a dispute route for premium audio claims.
- Canva Popular Music: personal use only, even on Canva Pro. A voiced Edlintics reel must use "Commercial use allowed" tracks only.
- TikTok CML: in-app use on TikTok only. Plan a separate export without music for TikTok, or a self-licensed track after Music Usage Confirmation.
- Platform audio libraries (Instagram in-app music, TikTok general library): personal use, not for a business account.
- CC BY attribution (MacLeod, Silverman): where to place it is the open question. MacLeod's rule is that the credit be easy to find; for platforms with no description (Reels caption works, X/Threads short) the cleanest option is the caption on every post. Pixabay avoids this issue entirely.
- NC licences: any CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA or ND track is excluded. Check the licence on every FMA and MacLeod track.
- Modern songs and covers: not safe, as above.
- Voice at 100, music at volume 8 does not remove the need for a licence. Content ID matches low-volume audio.
- AI-generated music pages (e.g. ElevenLabs, BudgetPixel in search results): not assessed, and not recommended without checking their terms.
- Source quality: many claims about TikTok, Meta and Canva come from music-licensing vendors with a commercial interest. Primary pages should be re-checked before December.

## 5. Implications for Edlintics

- Default plan: Canva "Commercial use allowed" stock Christmas piano at volume 8 under voice. Search terms to try in Canva Audio (untested, my suggestion): "christmas piano", "christmas calm", "holiday acoustic", "winter ambient", and filter out any result labelled Popular Music.
- Backup: Pixabay solo-piano tracks above, downloaded and imported into Canva as an upload. Log the licence with each use.
- Recognisable public-domain melody (Silent Night, Angels We Have Heard on High) suits a tone of calm seriousness better than jingle-bell sounds.
- December is a demand spike for exams, so the music should not distract from the topic. Volume 8 and a slow piano track match the brand voice.

## 6. What I did NOT investigate

- Canva's actual Christmas catalogue and any Content ID claims on specific Canva tracks. No public list found.
- YouTube Audio Library Christmas tracks, and whether its licence extends beyond YouTube.
- Specific Meta Sound Collection, FMA, Musopen and MacLeod Christmas tracks. Pages blocked or not retrievable.
- Whether Pixabay's Content ID registration has produced claims against business accounts.
- Listening quality of any shortlisted track. Nobody has listened to them yet.
- Public domain status in the EU or Vietnam (where publishing happens from). I looked at US-oriented sources only.
- Sound effects (sleigh bells, fireplace), Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Soundstripe or other paid libraries.
- Nutcracker recordings. No safe, verified recording found.
