Who we are: short demo videos that show Edlintics in use

Hoang asked for variations of three reference videos (a Brilliant TV spot and two Duolingo films) so the market learns what Edlintics is. Below: what the references do, where we fit, how we say who we are, and five video concepts we can make with our own engine and real screen recordings.

1. What the three references do

Brilliant · TV spot · 28 s

A hand plays with the lesson

  • White background, one cartoon hand that drags, turns and taps the real lesson visuals
  • Subject name on top changes fast: Geometry, Calculus, Pythagorean theorem
  • The speed of the cuts is the message: thousands of interactive lessons
  • One lesson card with its Continue button proves it is a real product
  • End: "Get started for free" and a URL pill
Duolingo · Learn chess · 25 s

The mascot meets the app

  • Mascot in a spotlight opens it, a character plays the new subject
  • A puzzle board, then the real app screen inside a phone
  • The mascot reacts, so the brand has a face and a joke
  • End card: logo, one promise line, store badges
Duolingo · Duocon teaser · 30 s

A camera flies through screens

  • Flat vector world, no cuts: the camera passes through phone screens into new scenes
  • Each scene is a different subject or place, a big friendly cast
  • Lesson cards and screens float by as proof
  • End: the whole cast, the logo and a date

Read by the lead from frames of each video on 18 Nov. We copy the patterns, never their scripts, characters or look.

2. Where Edlintics fits

The lane is open

  • Brilliant calls itself a visual, interactive tutor for maths and coding. Its course list has no accounting, finance or economics
  • CFI and Wall Street Prep sell job certification for investment banking and corporate finance, not first-year fundamentals
  • Khan Academy is free and broad; StudySmarter turns notes into flashcards
  • In the scan we found no interactive, bite-sized fundamentals product for business students. The scan was limited (sources in docs/research/2026-11-18-brand-demo-ads.md)

What we can show today, signed out

  • About 1,000 glossary terms, each with a "Check yourself" practice card
  • 9 calculators with live inputs
  • Chart of accounts with IFRS and US GAAP filters
  • Markets: bond table, map and yield curve
  • 14 courses in the catalogue, all "Coming soon": no price, date or sale may be promised yet
  • Note: the homepage hero cards are pictures, not tappable, so we film the glossary and calculators instead

3. How we say who we are

Taken from our own About page (edlintics.com/en/about), so every video says the same thing the website says. No user or student numbers (Hoang, 18 Nov).

Edlintics helps people learn business skills that apply to real work, in bitesized lessons that are enjoyable to work through

The story in one breath (for the voice)

Maths, coding and engineering have been rebuilt into short interactive lessons, and business simply had not. So we started building the courses we wished we could teach from, grounded in the forms and documents people really do have to fill in and balance

From the About page. It is also the bridge to the references: Brilliant did it for maths, we do it for business, without naming them

4. Five video concepts

A · after Brilliant · 25-30 s

"Ledger's hand": four subjects, four taps

White background, Ledger's flat hand works our real cards, subject chip on top changes with each one.

  1. Accounting: tap the right journal entry, it turns green (glossary card, real recording)
  2. Economics: drag demand right, the price rises (our supply and demand graph)
  3. Finance: type a price and a cost into a calculator, the margin updates live
  4. Statistics: a card on the average vs the median
  5. Line: "About 1,000 business terms, each with a card you can try"
  6. End: "Understand the process. Question the result" and an edlintics.com pill

"Ledger meets a hard word"

A short Ledger story in the flat world, then the real product solves it.

C · after the Duocon teaser · 30 s

"Through the screen": the brand film

One flat camera move, no cuts. Each phone screen opens into a world where a subject is used at work.

  3. A trading screen: the bond yield curve moves (finance, our Markets page)
  4. A factory: units up, cost per unit down (economies of scale)
  5. A city skyline from the career-cities maps (where these skills are used)
  6. Voice over the flight: maths and coding were rebuilt into short interactive lessons, and business had not, so we built it
  7. Out of the screen: the cast, the logo, "Create the next generation of business builders"

  3. The green state, one sentence that sticks
  4. "Try this card free on edlintics.com"
